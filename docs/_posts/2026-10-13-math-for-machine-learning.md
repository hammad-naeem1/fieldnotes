Machine learning uses mathematics, but you do not need to finish years of advanced math before starting a small project. It is more useful to learn a foundation, try a model, and return to the math when a concept becomes relevant.

## Start with algebra and functions

Be comfortable rearranging equations, reading a graph, working with ratios and percentages, and understanding a function as a rule that maps inputs to outputs. These basics help when you read formulas, inspect data, or compare predictions.

## Learn statistics and probability

For data science, begin with averages, spread, distributions, sampling, and the difference between correlation and causation. Then study probability, conditional probability, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests. These ideas help you reason about noisy data and uncertainty.

The [Khan Academy Statistics and Probability course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability) has lessons and practice exercises. [StatQuest](https://statquest.org/video_index.html) is useful when you want a short visual explanation of one statistics or ML concept.

## Build linear algebra intuition

Vectors and matrices provide a compact way to represent data and model computations. You can begin with vectors, dot products, matrix multiplication, and transformations. The [3Blue1Brown linear algebra lessons](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/linear-algebra) build visual intuition before you focus on notation.

## Add calculus when it helps explain learning

Derivatives describe how a quantity changes. In neural networks, gradients help update model parameters to reduce an error measure. Learn the idea of a slope and a derivative, then connect it to gradient descent when you study neural networks. You do not need to master every calculus topic before training a beginner model.

## A useful study order

1. Algebra, functions, graphs, and percentages.
2. Descriptive statistics and probability.
3. Vectors and matrices.
4. Derivatives and gradients as you reach deep learning.
5. Discrete math and logic for algorithms and computer science.

For a deeper reference, [Mathematics for Machine Learning](https://mml-book.github.io/book/mml-book.pdf) is available online. It covers linear algebra, calculus, probability, and optimization; it is best used as a reference after you have the basic algebra and statistics in place. You can also explore [MIT’s Mathematics for Computer Science course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/) for logic, proofs, graphs, counting, and discrete probability.

The aim is not to collect formulas. Try to explain what a concept means, work through a small example, and notice where it appears in a model or analysis.
